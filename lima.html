Delta One, ATL to LIM↗The flight

LeisureGood, not especially local

California to Atlanta to Jorge Chávez International

Two legs. West coast to Atlanta, then Atlanta straight down to Lima. Delta One is the lie flat cabin, which matters more than usual on this route because the Lima leg lands you into a full day you are expected to function in.

Fun fact

Lima runs on UTC-5 year round with no daylight saving, so from the west coast it is only a two hour shift. There is no real jet lag on this trip. What wrecks you is the flight length, not the clock.

Must know

Jorge Chávez opened an entirely new terminal in June 2025 and it is still shaking out the kinks. Build a real buffer on connections, and check your flight status the night before and the morning of.

On arrival

Getting out of the airport↗Transport

LeisureGood, not especially local

Jorge Chávez International to Miraflores

Roughly 45 to 90 minutes to Miraflores depending on traffic. Here is the part worth writing down: the hotel desk quoted us about $35 for a taxi. The Uber was about $17 for the same ride. Same road, same traffic, half the money.

Take the Uber. It picks up from the designated zone at Parking E1, not curbside, so walk out and follow the signs rather than standing at arrivals wondering why nobody is coming. Cabify works the same way.

Nearly every quote you get at a desk or counter is priced for someone who has not opened the app yet. It is not a scam, it is just the tourist rate, and it roughly doubles the fare on this exact route.

Must know

If you do want a taxi, use the staffed counters inside Arrivals and confirm the fare is todo incluido before getting in. Never take a ride from someone who approaches you outside Arrivals. That is the one real scam at this airport and it runs constantly.

Costa del Sol Wyndham Lima Airport↗Honest review

LeisureGood, not especially local

Inside Jorge Chávez International, Callao

The famous airport hotel, and the only one physically connected to the terminal. We booked it for the arrival night on the theory that landing tired and walking straight to a bed was the smart play. The service was genuinely good and the room was not. Both of those things are true and it would be unfair to only say one of them.

What was actually good

You walk to your gate. Plus a free shuttle every thirty minutes, a welcome drink on arrival, a restaurant open around the clock, and a free buffet breakfast we absolutely took advantage of before we left. For an airport hotel that is a real package, and the staff were fine.

Why we left anyway

The room was old in the way you feel rather than see in photos. Rust, a smell, and that sticky, been-here-too-long surface feel. After two flights and a full travel day, that is the wrong first night. We ate the free breakfast and got out on the next thing smoking.

The honest verdict

Worth it for a genuine tight connection, where being able to walk to the terminal and eat at 4 AM is the entire product. Not worth it as the first night of a real trip. A late arrival is not a reason. Take the 45 minute ride and wake up in Miraflores. Note the tiers too: the Grand rooms and the standard rooms are not the same hotel, and we were clearly not in the newer stock.

Av. José Larco at Av. Diagonal, Miraflores

This is the calmest cat colony you will ever walk through. My mother is genuinely scared of them and she walked the whole park. About the chicha morada

It is the purple one, and it is not a soft drink invention. It is pre-Columbian: purple corn boiled with pineapple rind, cinnamon, clove and quince, then cooled and finished with lime and sugar. Every Peruvian household has a version. Order it once even if you order nothing else.

Must know

Go early, not late. They close mid afternoon and when the fish is gone it is gone. There is no waiting area, no table service and no reservations. You stand until a stool opens, you eat, you leave. Cash is easiest. It is a five minute drive from Miraflores and the single best value on this entire page.

What is actually happening

Lima sits in a coastal desert that essentially never rains, and from roughly May to November it lives under a permanent low fog called the garúa. It does not fall as rain, it just hangs there. The result is a city that is simultaneously bone dry and constantly damp, sunless for weeks, and cold enough for a jacket at midday even though the thermometer says sixteen degrees.

Pack this instead

Layers and a light waterproof shell, not shorts. Long trousers, a jumper, closed shoes. Add one warm layer for the malecón, because the cliff runs several degrees colder than three blocks inland and the wind comes straight off the Pacific. Nothing you own needs to be heavy, but nothing should assume sun.

If heat is the point of your trip

Peruvian summer is December to March. That is when Lima is hot and the sky is actually blue. September is the tail end of winter. Every photograph of Lima you have ever seen was taken in January, which is exactly why this keeps happening to people. Herman Melville called Lima the saddest city in the world because of that veil of fog, and he was here in the same season.

What to actually order in Peru

Fifteen words of a
Peruvian menu.

Heat is marked, because ají is used constantly here and some of it lands harder than it looks.

CevicheHas heat
Raw fish cured in lime with red onion, chili and salt, served with sweet potato and giant corn. The lime liquid left in the bowl is leche de tigre and you drink it. Ask for it sin ají if anyone wants it mild.
TiraditoMilder
Ceviche's calmer cousin. Fish sliced thin like sashimi, sauced rather than marinated, no onion. This is the one to order for a parent who is unsure about raw fish.
CausaMild
Chilled layers of yellow potato whipped with lime and ají amarillo, packed around chicken, tuna or crab. Looks like a small cake. Tastes nothing like one.
Lomo saltadoMild
Beef stir fried with onion, tomato and soy in a screaming hot wok, served over fries and rice. Chinese technique, Peruvian ingredients. The safest bet on any menu.
Ají de gallinaGentle
Shredded chicken in a creamy yellow chili and walnut sauce over rice. Comfort food, warm rather than hot.
AnticuchosHas heat
Beef heart, marinated and grilled on skewers over coals. It does not taste like organ meat, it tastes like the best steak tip you have had. The street food of Lima.
Rocoto rellenoGenuinely spicy
A rocoto pepper stuffed with spiced beef and cheese and baked. The pepper is the whole dish and it is hot. Order one for the table to try, not one each.
Papa a la huancaínaMild
Boiled potato under a cold, creamy cheese and yellow chili sauce. Almost every meal starts with this somewhere.
Arroz chaufaMild
Peruvian Chinese fried rice, born in Lima's Barrio Chino. Order it at a chifa and understand half of Lima instantly.
Pan con chicharrónMild
Fried pork, sweet potato and salsa criolla in a roll. The national hangover breakfast, sold before noon and rarely after.
PicaronesSweet
Squash and sweet potato doughnut rings, fried and drenched in chancaca syrup. Street dessert. Eat them standing up.
Suspiro a la limeñaSweet
Caramel manjar blanco under a port spiked meringue. The name means the sigh of a woman from Lima, which tells you exactly how sweet it is.
Pisco sourStrong
Pisco, lime, syrup, egg white, bitters. Peru's national drink and a genuine argument with Chile over who invented it. Stronger than it tastes.

Pisco, ginger ale, lime, ice. Lighter than a pisco sour and much easier to drink with food. This is what locals order on a weeknight.

Access runs through a private condominium development, departures are limited and decided by them, and multiple visitors report that the tour includes a pitch to sell you a plot of land. With parents and three days left, this is a no.

If the sandboarding is the part you want, the Chilca dunes are about an hour south of Miraflores, tours run at 8 AM and 2 PM daily, and operators will dial the dune buggy down to a gentle version on request. That is a half day instead of a full one.

Dansa, the Wankar dinner theatre

Nine courses with live dancers and musicians, and it was the anchor of the original Thursday. It only runs Thursday to Saturday, and Thursday is now a 3 AM airport run. Wednesday is not an option for it. Genuinely the biggest loss on this trip.

Book it first thing on the next Lima trip. Joinnus, or +51 1 219 9000, and ask for platea on the ground floor.

Parque de las Leyendas and the Machu Picchu replica

A ten metre Machu Picchu replica in a cave you reach by boat, plus a full zoo, and seniors pay S/4 to get in. It is also 7.3 miles out and needs two and a half hours, which is the entire morning that Huaca Pucllana now has to occupy because Huaca closes Tuesdays.

Distance lost this one, not quality. It is the first thing I would add back with one more day.

Al Toke Pez and La Mar

Both are lunch only, and there is exactly one lunch slot left. Horneando Ando took it because it sits four minutes from the Barranco walk, which saves a crossing of the city on the tightest day of the trip.

Al Toke Pez was always the rough one anyway: eight stools, a sidewalk queue and nowhere to sit while you wait.

Clon, Barranco

A good dinner that lost its night to Astrid y Gastón. With one evening left, the flagship wins.

La Picantería, Surquillo

Héctor Solís's whole-fish picantería was the plan for Thursday lunch, and it is closed. The Surquillo location shut in May 2026. Solís has said it returns in 2027 in a different district. Every listing still online shows it open, which is how a trip loses an afternoon.

Replaced with La Mar on Av. La Mar, eight minutes from the hotel. Same job on the day, a proper sit-down seafood lunch, and the one place in Lima that arguably did more than any other to put Peruvian food on the map.

Helarte

Calle Bolívar 205, open daily 8 AM to 10 PM, and you already went. Worth knowing it is 0.4 miles from the lobby and open until 10 PM, which makes it the default late dessert on any night that ends early. Lúcuma is the flavor to get if you did not.

Money, and the ATM trap

About S/3.37 to the dollar. Every price on this page is in soles first, because that is the money you will actually be handing over.

Do not pull cash at the airport. The airport ATMs charged us close to $8 per withdrawal. Wait until Miraflores and use a bank machine, BCP, Interbank, Scotiabank or BBVA, inside the branch rather than a standalone kiosk.

One button that saves you money

When any ATM or card reader offers to charge you in dollars instead of soles, always choose soles. That offer is called dynamic currency conversion and the exchange rate baked into it is terrible.

Take out one larger amount rather than three small ones, since the fee is per withdrawal, not per sol. Tipping is about 10 percent, and check the bill for servicio first, because if it is there you do not add more.

Water

Do not drink the tap water, including in the hotel. Sealed bottles only. Ice at real restaurants is fine. At street stalls and juice carts, ask for it without ice.

Getting around

Uber and Cabify both work well here and are cheap. Book through the app rather than hailing on the street, especially at night and especially outside a busy attraction.

Food delivery, the trap

Download Rappi first and PedidosYa as the backup. Do it at the airport on wifi, not at 10 PM when you are hungry.
