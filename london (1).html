The UK ETACannot move

US citizens have needed a UK Electronic Travel Authorisation since 25 February 2026. Without the approval email the airline can refuse to board you, and that is the end of the trip before it starts.

Must know

Apply only at gov.uk/eta or the official UK ETA app. Every other site charges more for the same thing and cannot make it faster. The app scans your passport and face with your phone, which beats the web form. The ETA is tied to the passport you apply with, so use the one you are flying on.

What it really cost

The site says £20. It landed on my card at $28 after taxes and conversion. Budget the dollar figure, not the pound one, because that gap shows up on every British price on this page. The US Embassy in London is blunter about the rule than gov.uk is. Its wording is that without evidence of an approved ETA you may be denied boarding by your airline or not allowed entry. Do not fly on a pending application.

The only fare fact you need

Your entire day of travel is capped at £16.30. You touch zone 6 twice, Heathrow in and Heathrow out, so you will hit the zones 1 to 6 cap and everything in between is free after that. Stop adding up individual fares. Tap, travel, and the system stops charging you at £16.30. The zones 1 to 2 cap is £8.90 if you somehow never leave the middle. Heathrow Express is excluded from the cap, which is another reason not to take it.

Must know

Transport for London says plainly that some cards issued in the USA are not accepted, specifically certain Mastercard and Maestro cards, and some non-UK Visa cards. American Express is listed as accepted. Test your card at the very first gate at Heathrow while you still have the ticket machines next to you.

Do this

Tap in and out with the same card or the same phone every single time. Mix them and the daily cap will not apply and you will pay full fare on every leg. Your bank may add a foreign transaction fee, which is a card question, not a London one.

Fun fact

You do not need an Oyster card and you do not need an app. A contactless bank card or phone is the whole system. Londoners stopped buying paper tickets years ago.

Citymapper first

This is the app Londoners actually use, and it beats Google Maps underground. It tells you which part of the train to board so you come out at the right exit, offers alternatives the moment a line goes down, and has a get-me-home button. Free, iOS and Android. Offline it keeps your starred journeys, scheduled Tube times and the Tube map, so star your four journeys at home while you still have wifi.

TfL Go, and download your maps

TfL Go is the official Transport for London app: live Tube status, journey planner, step-free routes. Offline it gives you the Tube map and nothing else. Then open Google Maps, search London, and download the offline area covering Bloomsbury, Euston and the South Bank. Walking directions are what you will actually need on Malet Street in the dark.

what3words, and the solo part

what3words gives every three metre square on earth a three word address and works without a data connection, using GPS alone. It is free. That is the app you want if you are turned around at eleven at night and need to tell somebody exactly where you are. The UK emergency number is 999, and 112 also works. Turn on location sharing with one person at home before you fly, and send them your flight number and both event addresses.

The two tickets and the one card

Your Action on Male Suicide ticket lives in Eventbrite and your Mad Movements ticket lives in Luma. Open both apps at home, screenshot both tickets, and keep the screenshots in your photos. Signal underground is unreliable and a ticket you cannot load is a ticket you do not have. Same for your boarding pass and the ETA approval email.

Data, and the thing that ruins all of this

None of these apps help you if your phone has no signal. Sort an international plan or an eSIM before you fly, not at Heathrow. And put the contactless card you will tap into your Apple or Google Wallet as the default card, so you are not fishing in a bag at a gate with people behind you.
